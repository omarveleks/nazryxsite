Why manufacturers come to us

Most export failures are decided before the first shipment.

Not by the product, and rarely by the price. By choosing a market on a brochure, a partner on whoever answered the email, and a registration pathway after the product was already picked.

Partner The distributor was chosen because they replied

Responsiveness at the enquiry stage tells you nothing about whether a company sells your category, calls on the right prescribers, or already carries three competing lines it would rather push.

Evidence The market was assessed from a catalogue

Whether a molecule is crowded or wide open is a fact sitting in the national register. Entering without reading it is how a company files into a category with forty suppliers and misses the one next door with none.

Sequence Registration is discovered, not planned

The product is chosen, then the pathway is investigated. Reversed, whole categories open up that looked closed — and the dead ends are spotted before money moves.

Control After the agency is signed, visibility stops

Orders arrive or they do not, and nobody can tell you why. Who registered against you, what the shelf price became, whether the distributor is still pushing the line — all of it goes dark.

What we run

Eight functions, from first market read to steady reorders.

We are your commercial and regulatory arm in these markets, not a broker who makes an introduction and disappears. Everything below is work our team carries out on your portfolio.

01

Finding great distribution partners

We do not send you a directory. Every distributor in our markets is profiled from the register itself — what they actually hold, in which categories, from which manufacturers — so a shortlist is built on evidence rather than on who has a website.

  • Ranked by real portfolio overlap with your products
  • Category strength and national standing, not self-description
  • Who they already represent, and where you would conflict
  • Warm introduction and negotiation support through to agency
02

Building and analysing your portfolio

Which of your existing products belong in which market, in what order, and what you should add. We map your catalogue against each market's register and essential medicines list and rank it by what is genuinely open.

  • Your lines scored per market on supplier depth and demand
  • The thin positions where you would set price rather than match it
  • Gaps worth developing, with the volume signal behind each
  • Products to leave out, and the reason, in writing
03

Regulatory checking and registration

Site registration, product dossier, pathway and timeline — established before you commit, then carried through to submission. In several of our markets the plant must be approved before a single product can be filed, and that order catches people out.

  • Country-by-country route, requirement and realistic timeline
  • Dossier compiled, gaps in your existing pack identified early
  • Zone IVb stability and labelling requirements checked up front
  • Agency documentation, legalisation and filing handled locally
04

Quality and compliance

Buyers in these markets are increasingly unwilling to take a GMP certificate on trust, and regulators are tightening. We make sure what you present stands up before it is in front of either.

  • GMP, CPP and certification pack reviewed against each market's rules
  • Animal-origin, halal and excipient declarations prepared where required
  • Batch documentation and CoA formats aligned to local expectations
  • Queries from the regulator handled rather than forwarded
05

Pricing and positioning

What a line can actually be sold for, per market, per pack — taken from registered prices and from what competing products are really quoting, not from a target margin worked backwards.

  • Landed and shelf price benchmarks against real competitors
  • Where you hold leverage and where you would be undercut
  • Pack and presentation choices that change the price bracket
  • Tender and institutional pricing where the market is programme-led
06

Sales and marketing growth

A registration is not a sale. We work with the distributor after launch on the things that decide whether your line moves: who is detailing it, to whom, with what, and against what.

  • Launch planning with the distributor's own sales team
  • Promotional material and product training prepared locally
  • Target prescriber and institution mapping by geography
  • Reorder tracking, with the diagnosis when a line slows
07

Market and demand intelligence

The same engine we run for distributors, pointed at your side of the table. What is being granted, to whom, in your categories — every week, per market.

  • New registrations in your categories, and who took them
  • Competitors entering or lapsing out of a molecule
  • Essential medicines list changes and procurement signals
  • Renewal dates tracked ahead of the deadline, not after it
08

Account management and reporting

One point of contact for every market, and a written view of what happened in each one. You should not have to chase four distributors in three time zones to know how your export business is doing.

  • Weekly written reporting per market
  • Order, stock and reorder visibility from the distributor side
  • Quarterly review of what to push, fix or drop
  • Escalation handled by us, in market, in person where needed

We are on the ground, and we are accountable for the outcome.

A broker earns on the introduction and has no stake in what happens next. We earn when your product actually sells, which means our interest and yours point the same way from the first call. If a line is not going to work in a market, you will hear that from us early — it costs us less than selling you a year of effort that goes nowhere.

What this looks like in practice

Twelve ophthalmic products. No supplier in the country.

Reading one market's full register against its national essential medicines list, we found twelve ophthalmic molecules with no human-medicine registration in the country at all — not a thin market, an empty one. Glaucoma drops, diagnostic mydriatics, topical anaesthetics, an antifungal for a condition that blinds people there every year.

Weeks earlier, one of the larger distributors in that market had asked us for one of those twelve by name, unprompted. Demand on one side, nothing registered on the other, and no way for either party to see the other without reading 9,000 certificates line by line.

For a manufacturer with a sterile ophthalmic line, that is not a lead. It is a category with no incumbent to displace and a buyer already asking. That is the kind of position we are looking for on your portfolio.

Control

Who holds the registration?

It is the question that decides everything else, and it is usually left until the paperwork is already in front of you. Here is the plain version before you ask.

Stays yours You

The product, the dossier, the plant and the brand are yours throughout. Nothing in how we work changes that, in any market.

The part that matters The registration holder

In most of our markets the certificate names a local party, and whoever is named controls that product on the ground. Who it should be differs by country — and it is decided before filing, not after.

Replaceable Your distributor

Stocks it, sells it, details it. If they stop performing they can be changed — provided the step to their left was set up with that in mind.

The registration never goes in Nazryx’s name. We are your agent, not a party that holds your product hostage. Before anything is filed we tell you who should hold it in that market, and exactly what changing partner would involve later.

Questions

The things manufacturers ask on the first call.

Are you an agent, a consultant, or a distributor?

We act as your trade and regulatory arm in the markets we cover. We do not buy and resell your product, and we do not take title to stock. We find and manage the distributor who does, and we run the registration and market work around it.

In practice that means you get the control of a direct relationship with the distributor, and we are the party accountable for the market working.

How are you paid?

Commission on business that actually moves, agreed per market before anything starts. Where a registration programme requires sustained regulatory work ahead of any revenue, that is scoped and agreed separately — we will not dress up a year of filing work as free.

What we will not do is charge for an introduction. If nothing sells, we have not earned.

We already have a distributor in that market.

Then the useful question is whether they are the right one, and that is answerable from the register rather than from the relationship. We can show you where they actually rank in your categories, what else they carry, and whether your line is a priority for them or a shelf filler.

Often the answer is that the distributor is fine and the problem is elsewhere — pricing, registration timing, or nobody detailing the product. We would rather fix that than replace them.

How long does registration actually take?

It varies by market and we will give you a real number per country rather than an average. What is consistent is the sequence: in several of our markets the manufacturing site has to be registered before any product from it can be filed, so a plant that is not yet approved is the first thing to fix, not the dossier.

We tell you the route and the timeline before you spend anything on it.

What do you need from us to start?

Your product list with dosage forms and strengths, your GMP and certification pack, the markets you already export to, and one person we can ask questions of. That is enough for the market read.

Dossiers and legalised documents come later, and only for the products you decide to take forward.
