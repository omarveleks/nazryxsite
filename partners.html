Why companies come to us

Most sourcing decisions are made on last year's information.

None of these are failures of effort. They are what happens when the work of gathering, checking and connecting information has no owner — so it gets done in the gaps between everything else, by people whose actual job is selling.

Time Every supplier approach is evaluated from scratch

Dozens of inbound offers a month, each needing dossier review, pricing checks and back-and-forth. Most go nowhere, and nothing learned is kept for the next one.

Margin Underperforming products get dropped, not diagnosed

A line stops moving and comes off the list. Whether the cause was pricing, competition, registration timing or promotion is rarely established, so the same mistake repeats in the next category.

Cash Stock decisions run ahead of demand evidence

Overstock ties up working capital; stockouts hand the shelf to a competitor. Both are forecasting problems, and forecasting is a data problem before it is a judgement one.

Risk Registration timelines are discovered, not planned

A product is chosen, then the regulatory pathway is investigated. When that order is reversed, whole categories open up that looked closed — and dead ends get spotted before money moves.

What this looks like in practice

Fifty-three registrations. Not one of them usable.

Screening a market for a client, we checked a widely used antiparasitic against the national register. It came back with 53 active registrations — on paper, a saturated molecule nobody should enter.

Every single one was veterinary. There was no human-medicine registration in the country at all, against a substantial disease burden. What read as the most crowded line in the category was the most open position on the list.

That distinction is four minutes of work if you know to look for it, and invisible if you don't. It is the difference between a supplier list and an analysis — and it is the kind of thing we are looking for on your catalogue every week.

Questions

The things people ask on the first call.

Is this software, or people?

Both, and the order matters. We built the software because the analysis is not possible by hand at this scale. But you are not buying a login — our team operates it, and what reaches you is worked output rather than a screen you have to interpret.

The portal exists so your team can see and use that output. It is not the product.

What do you need from us, realistically?

One person, for about a week, at onboarding. Your product catalogue, supplier list, the markets you sell into and your category priorities. Most of it is exports you already have; none of it needs to be tidy first.

After onboarding, the demand on your side drops sharply — a weekly read and a call when something needs a decision.

How is this different from a market research report?

A report describes a market on the day it was written and then starts ageing. Registers change, suppliers exit, registrations lapse, tenders open.

We are not producing a document. We are watching a specific catalogue against live sources and telling you when something moves — and then doing the sourcing and quotation work that follows.

Do you replace our sales team?

No, and we would be suspicious of anyone claiming to. We take the research, qualification and comparison work off them so they spend their time in front of buyers instead of in spreadsheets.

What happens to our data?

Your catalogue, supplier lists and buyer data are used for your partnership and nothing else. They are not pooled, resold, or used to advise anyone else. Terms are written into the agreement rather than left to trust.

Which markets do you cover?

We work across seven distribution markets in Africa and Asia today, with partner offices in Lahore, London and Almaty. Sourcing runs through five manufacturing markets — Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, Malaysia and Russia — with others on a need basis where a molecule or a certification calls for it.

If your market is not one we cover, we will tell you on the first call.

Why is intake limited?

Because the model is tuned per catalogue and the work is done by people. A partnership that is spread thin is not worth having on either side, so we hold a small number at a time and take on new ones when there is genuine capacity.
